Guess the Other Coin

Two players each toss a fair coin, separately. Each sees only their own coin, then guesses the other's. They win together if at least one guess is right and lose if both are wrong. They can agree a plan beforehand but cannot talk after the toss. Is there a plan that wins every time?

Every toss

Every plan

Click a cell to load that pair of plans into the toss above.

A player's guess can only depend on their own coin, so each has just four plans: say the same, say the opposite, always say heads or always say tails. A random plan is a mix of these four, so it can't beat the best of them. Of the 16 pairs, two win every toss: the two ways to split Match and No match.

As a strategy game. The players share one payoff, so this is a team game with hidden information: each plan is a strategy chosen before the toss. Six pairs are stable, or Nash equilibria: the two that never lose, and the four where both always name a fixed face. Those four win only 75%, yet no player can do better by changing plan alone. Being stable isn't enough, so the team has to agree on the hedge before the coins are tossed.

Why it works

Count the right guesses.

Each guess is right exactly half the time, whatever the plan. A is guessing B's coin, which A never sees and which doesn't depend on A's coin. So across the four tosses, any plan of A's is right in exactly two, and the same goes for B. That makes four right guesses in all, whatever the players agree.

Winning every toss needs exactly one right guess per toss. There are four tosses and only four right guesses to share out. Every toss needs at least one, so no toss can have two: the guesses must never be right at the same time.

The change of view finds that split. As bets on the pair of coins, the two guesses must be complementary events. Match and No match is the only event both players can name, so A takes one side and B the other. When both say “same”, they back the same side: right together on HH and TT, wrong together on HT and TH, and only half the tosses are won.

The quant lesson is a hedge. You can't make either bet better than 50%, but by taking opposite sides of one event the two payoffs become perfectly negatively correlated, and the team's total is fixed at one right guess on every toss.

The Python solver in solver.py and the tests in test/test_solver.py verify:

  • AnswerA “same”, B “opposite” wins all four tosses, with exactly one right guess each time.
  • UniquenessOf the 16 pairs of plans, only same/opposite and opposite/same never lose.
  • Half rightFor every pair, each player is right in exactly two of the four tosses.
  • Win ratesSame/same wins 50%; always heads/always heads wins 75%; no pair wins less than 50%.
