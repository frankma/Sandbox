GuanDan 掼蛋

A partnership climbing game from Jiangsu that spread through China's banks and brokerages in the mid-2010s and is now played everywhere. Two teams race to get rid of their cards and climb from level 2 to level A.

01The idea in one paragraph

You and the player opposite are a team. Someone leads a combination (a single, a pair, a straight and so on). Going round counter-clockwise, each player either beats it with a higher combination of the same shape, drops a bomb, or passes. When three players pass in a row, whoever played last wins the trick and leads anything they like. The first player to empty their hand comes first. How your partner finishes decides how many levels your team climbs.

It looks like Big Two or Dou Dizhu, with three twists. The level card changes every hand, two of its hearts are wild, and you are playing for a team rather than for yourself.

02Rank order and the level card

Both teams start at level 2. Each hand is played at the level of the team that won the last one. The current level's rank is the level card. It jumps above the ace, just under the two jokers. The two hearts of the level rank are wild and can stand for any card except a joker.

In straights, tubes and plates the level card goes back to its natural place, so at level 7 the run 5-6-7-8-9 still works. An ace can be high (10-J-Q-K-A) or low (A-2-3-4-5), but it can't sit in the middle of a run.

03The seven ordinary combinations

An ordinary combination can only be beaten by the same type with the same number of cards and a higher rank. You can never answer a pair with a triple, or a straight with a full house.

04Bombs beat everything ordinary

A bomb can be played on any trick at any time, even when it isn't your turn to lead. Only a bigger bomb beats it. Bombs are ranked first by type, from the bottom of this list to the top, then by rank within a type. A bomb of the level rank is the best of its size.

The odd one out is the straight flush. It sits between five and six of a kind, and it is ranked by its top card whatever the level.

If the player who won a trick has already gone out, their partner leads next. This is called jiē fēng (接风, catching the wind). It is a strong reason to help a partner who is down to a few cards.

06Finishing, climbing, and winning at A

The hand goes on until both players of one team are out. The team of the first player out wins the hand. Where the partner finishes sets the climb:

Finish of the winning teamChineseLevels upTribute next hand
1st and 2nd双上 · double win+3Both losers pay

1st and 4th平局 · narrow win+1Last-out player pays

Most Chinese club and tournament rules use +3/+2/+1, and this practice table does too. Some English write-ups give +4 for a double win.

  • The winning team's new level becomes the level for the next hand.
  • You can't skip past A. A team that reaches A must win a hand at level A, finishing 1st and 2nd or 1st and 3rd, to win the match. A 1st-and-4th finish leaves them on A.
  • Under many rules, a team that fails at A three times drops back to level 2.

07Tribute and return

From the second hand on, the losers pay before play starts. This is the part of the game that rewards a big win.

Step 1

Tribute · 进贡

The paying player hands over their single highest card, which can't be a wild heart. After a double win both losers pay, and the bigger tribute card goes to the first-out winner.

Step 2

Return · 还贡

Each receiver gives one card back to the player who paid. Common club rules say the returned card must be a 10 or lower.

Step 3

Resist · 抗贡

If the paying side holds both red jokers between them, tribute is cancelled. The previous first-out player then leads. Otherwise whoever paid the bigger tribute leads.

08How good players think

Plan the whole hand

Before the first lead, split your 27 cards into the fewest combinations. Every combination is one turn you need to win. Long shapes (straights, tubes, plates, full houses) clear five or six cards in one go.

Count the big cards

Track the four jokers, the eight level cards and the aces. When the red jokers are gone, your black joker controls every single. The practice table keeps this count for you.

Bombs are for control

Don't break a bomb to make a pair. Spend bombs to stop an opponent who is about to go out, or to take the lead when your remaining hand finishes in one or two turns.

Don't beat your partner

If partner is winning the trick, pass. Overtake only to go out yourself or to stop partner leading into a weak spot.

Lead what you want back

Lead your weakest shape. Watch what the opponents pass on: a pass over a pair of 8s suggests they have few spare pairs above 8.

Feed a short partner

When partner is down to a few cards, lead the shape they can beat, usually a low single or pair. Their finishing first turns your hand into a likely +2 or +3.
