Honor or Grab

Several teams strike a deal with every other team, round after round. In every round each team decides, rival by rival, whether to honor that deal or grab. Grabbing always pays more on its own, but if everyone grabs, everyone loses.

Both honor+6 each
One grabs, one honors+14 / −6
Both grab−4 each
Every decision is grab−12 each
Every decision is honor+15 each
Bonus roundall scores ×3

A round is a decision matrix: each team makes one decision toward each other team, and every pair of teams settles its deal from the two decisions between them. A team's round score is the sum of its deals. When every decision in the matrix is the same, the −12 or +15 rule replaces the sums. Bonus rounds come at random and are announced before teams decide.

One round: grabbing wins

Each deal on its own is a prisoner's dilemma. Whatever the rival decides toward you, grabbing pays more: 14 instead of 6 if they honor, and −4 instead of −6 if they grab.

Many rounds: the shadow of the future

With a fixed, known number of rounds, cooperation unravels from the back. Everyone grabs in the last round because nothing follows it. That makes the second-to-last round effectively the last, and so on back to round one.

Cooperation can hold when the end is uncertain. Suppose that after each round the game goes on with probability \(\delta\), and a team you grab grabs you for good, while the others keep honoring you. Grabbing is then a trade: a one-off gain now against a loss in every round after.

Strategy tournament

Eight strategies play every possible line-up of teams, with random bonus rounds, many times over. Each strategy decides separately toward every rival, so reciprocal strategies can reward one team and punish another in the same round.
