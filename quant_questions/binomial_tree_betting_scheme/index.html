Binomial Tree Betting Scheme

Your team meets your uncle's team in the World Series, and the first side to four wins takes it. Before each game you name a stake \(b\). Your uncle writes you an IOU for \(b\) if your team wins the game, and you write him one if it loses. The IOUs are settled in cash when the series ends.

You want to walk away exactly $100 up if your team wins the series and $100 down if it loses. How much do you bet on game one?

$31.25 Opening bet

This is \(100 \times \binom{6}{3}/2^6\): the payoff times the chance that a fair series goes to game seven. It doesn't depend on how good either team is.

Why it works

Let \(V(w,l)\) be the IOU balance you need on reaching record \(w\)–\(l\). At the end of the series it is fixed: \(V(4,l) = +100\) and \(V(w,4) = -100\).

From a live node you bet \(b\). A win takes you to \(V + b\) and a loss to \(V - b\), and both must land on the balance their next node needs:

$$V(w,l) = \frac{V(w{+}1,l) + V(w,l{+}1)}{2}, \qquad b(w,l) = \frac{V(w{+}1,l) - V(w,l{+}1)}{2}.$$

This is delta-hedging on a binomial tree. The even-money bet is the hedge, and the value is the average of its two children: the risk-neutral measure with \(p = \tfrac12\). The real chance of winning never enters, just as the real drift never enters a Black–Scholes price.

Working back from game seven: at 3–3 you bet the full $100. At 0–0 the two children are \(V(1,0) = +31.25\) and \(V(0,1) = -31.25\), so the opening bet is $31.25.

Bet by record, before the next game

Rows are your team's wins, columns its losses. The table is symmetric. The stakes grow as the series tightens and shrink when one side runs away with it.

The general result for a best-of-\((2n-1)\) series with payoff \(X\):

$$b(0,0) = X\binom{2n-2}{n-1}\Bigl(\tfrac12\Bigr)^{2n-1}, \qquad n = 4:\ 100 \cdot \tfrac{20}{64} = 31.25.$$

Checks

The Python solver in solver.py and its tests in test/test_solver.py verify:

  • AnswerThe opening bet is 31.25.
  • Every pathAll \(2^7\) win/loss sequences, played with these bets, finish at exactly +100 or −100, matching who won the series.
  • Symmetry\(V(w,l) = -V(l,w)\) and \(b(w,l) = b(l,w)\).
  • Closed form\(V(1,0) = 100\,(2 \cdot \tfrac{21}{32} - 1)\), where 21/32 is the fair-coin chance of winning the series from 1–0.
  • Game sevenAt 3–3 the bet is the full $100.
  • ScalingThe bet doubles with the payoff. Best-of-1 needs $100 and best-of-3 needs $50.
